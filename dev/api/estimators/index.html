CoordinateDescent — Module
CoordinateDescent

Lasso estimators computed by coordinate descent: lasso, sqrtLasso, scaledLasso, feasibleLasso!, LassoPath, the penalized generalized linear models glmLasso and GLMLassoPath, the group lasso groupLasso and GroupLassoPath, the multi-response lasso multiLasso and MultiLassoPath, the inference after selection of R's hdm (rlasso, postLasso, doubleSelection), and the varying-coefficient estimators locpoly and locpolyl1. They run the coordinate descent, the penalty paths and the reweighting of ProximalSolvers.jl on the losses and penalties of ProximalBase.jl, as does coordinateDescent!, which minimizes f(x) + g(x) for a loss f (a CoordinateDifferentiableFunction or a loss that ProximalSolvers knows) and a penalty g that separates across coordinates, such as the weighted ℓ1 norm NormL1. The lasso solvers accept real data and penalties of any type and convert them to a common floating point type: the element type of X and y promoted to a float, or that of the iterate passed to scaledLasso! and feasibleLasso!.

CoordinateDescent.LassoPath — Type
LassoPath(X, y, λpath, options=CDOptions(); max_hat_s=Inf, standardizeX=true, intercept=false, screening=true,
          gram=:auto, weights=nothing, offset=nothing, alpha=1, penalty=nothing, lower=-Inf, upper=Inf,
          rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)
LassoPath(X, y, options=CDOptions(); nλ=100, λminratio, kwargs...)

Lasso solutions β0path[k] and βpath[k] of

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + λpath[k] ∑ⱼ ωⱼ |βⱼ|

along a decreasing sequence λpath, each one warm started from the previous solution, by ProximalSolvers.solve_path. The intercept β₀ is not penalized, and it is zero unless intercept is true. The loadings are the standard deviations ωⱼ = sqrt(∑ᵢ (Xᵢⱼ - x̄ⱼ)² / n) of the columns when standardizeX is true (centered only with an intercept) and ωⱼ = 1 otherwise.

Without λpath, the path has nλ values spaced evenly on the log scale from λmax, the smallest value whose solution is zero, down to λminratio ⋅ λmax; λminratio defaults to 0.01 when X has more columns than rows and to 1e-4 otherwise. The first value is λmax times 1 + √eps, so that rounding cannot make the first solution nonzero.

With screening, each λ is solved first over the coordinates kept by the sequential strong rule of Tibshirani et al. (2012), and the optimality conditions of the others are checked afterwards; coordinates that violate them are added and the problem solved again. kktChecks[k] is the number of checks at λpath[k]: one when the rule kept every coordinate it should have, zero without screening. When options.warmStart is false, each value is solved from zero along its own path of options.numSteps levels, without screening.

The path stops early, after the first solution with more than max_hat_s nonzero coefficients; the returned λpath then contains only the values that were solved for. The solutions are stored as SparseVectors, so a path takes memory proportional to the number of nonzero coefficients. iterations[k] and converged[k] are the passes of coordinate descent at λpath[k] and whether it met its stopping rule. See also refitLassoPath.

The keyword arguments kwargs that are not listed are fields of CDOptions that replace those of options, and the random order of coordinate descent is drawn from rng. With gram, the path is solved on the Gram matrix of the columns, and with weights and offset the loss is the weighted one, as described for lasso; λmax and the loadings are weighted too. fit(LassoPath, X, y, ...) is the same as LassoPath(X, y, ...). The functions of StatsAPI apply to the path: coef, predict and nobs; nobs is the number of observations, also stored as nobs.

alpha and penalty give the elastic net and other penalties, as for lasso: LassoPath(X, y; alpha=0.5) is glmnet's elastic-net path at a fixed α, whose λmax is that of the lasso divided by α, and LassoPath(X, y; penalty=MCP(1, 3)) the path of ncvreg, on standardized columns with standardizeX. Along a path of a nonconvex penalty, each solution is the local one reached from the previous solution, starting from zero at λmax. penalty holds the penalty at level one, NormL1(1, ω) for the lasso with loadings ω; the solution at λpath[k] minimizes the loss plus that penalty at level λpath[k], applied to scales .* β when scales is not nothing (standardizeX with a penalty other than the ℓ1 norm). lower and upper bound the coefficients, as for lasso; penalty is then Constrained(g, IndBox(l, u)), and λmax is the smallest level whose solution is zero under the bounds.

CoordinateDescent.LassoSolution — Type
LassoSolution

Result returned by lasso, sqrtLasso, scaledLasso and feasibleLasso!.

Fields

  • x::SparseIterate: the estimated coefficients
  • residuals::Vector: y - intercept - X * x
  • penalty: the penalty at which x was computed, a NormL1 for the ℓ1 estimators, or the penalty of lasso's alpha or penalty at the level λ; with lower or upper, that penalty on the box of the bounds, Constrained(g, IndBox(l, u))
  • σ: an estimate of the noise level (see the documentation of each solver), or nothing
  • iterations::Int: passes of coordinate descent over the coordinates, summed over all the lasso fits the solver made
  • converged::Bool: whether coordinate descent met its stopping rule within maxIter passes and, for scaledLasso! and feasibleLasso!, the outer iteration met its stopping rule within its maxIter iterations
  • intercept: the unpenalized intercept, zero unless the solver was called with intercept = true
  • hasintercept::Bool: whether the solver fitted an intercept
  • weights: the observation weights of the fit, nothing for none
  • scales: nothing when penalty applies to x, or the scales s of the columns when it applies to s .* x, the coefficients of the standardized columns X[:, j] / s[j] (standardizeX with a penalty other than the ℓ1 norm)

The functions of StatsAPI apply to it: coef, predict, residuals, nobs, dof and deviance.

CoordinateDescent.feasibleLasso! — Method
feasibleLasso!(x, X, y, λ0, options=IterLassoOptions(); intercept=false, rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)

Heteroscedasticity-robust lasso of Belloni, Chen, Chernozhukov and Hansen (2012), which uses data-driven penalty loadings. It alternates between the lasso

β̂ = argmin  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + λ0 ∑ⱼ Γⱼ |βⱼ|

and the update of the loadings Γⱼ = sqrt(∑ᵢ (Xᵢⱼ - x̄ⱼ)² rᵢ² / n) from the residuals r = y - β̂₀ - Xβ̂, until the relative change in Γ is below options.optTol (by ProximalSolvers.reweight!), or warns after options.maxIter iterations. The intercept β₀ is not penalized; it and the column means x̄ⱼ are zero unless intercept is true.

The initial residuals are chosen by options.initProcedure (see IterLassoOptions); with :InitStd they come from a lasso with penalty λ0 σinit ∑ⱼ sⱼ |βⱼ|, sⱼ = sqrt(∑ᵢ (Xᵢⱼ - x̄ⱼ)² / n). Coordinate descent starts from and overwrites x, and each lasso fit is warm started from the previous one. Returns a LassoSolution whose penalty.w holds the loadings of the last fit and whose σ is the sample standard deviation of the residuals. The keyword arguments kwargs are fields of IterLassoOptions that replace those of options, as in feasibleLasso!(x, X, y, λ0; maxIter=50), and the random order of coordinate descent is drawn from rng.

CoordinateDescent.lasso — Method
lasso(X, y, λ, options=CDOptions(); intercept=false, standardizeX=false, gram=:auto, weights=nothing,
      offset=nothing, alpha=1, penalty=nothing, lower=-Inf, upper=Inf, rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)
lasso(X, y, λ, ω, options=CDOptions(); intercept=false, gram=:auto, weights=nothing, offset=nothing,
      lower=-Inf, upper=Inf, rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)

Solve the lasso

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + λ ∑ⱼ ωⱼ |βⱼ|

by coordinate descent. The intercept β₀ is not penalized, and it is zero unless intercept is true. In the first form the loadings are ωⱼ = 1, or with standardizeX the standard deviations sqrt(∑ᵢ (Xᵢⱼ - x̄ⱼ)² / n) of the columns, with x̄ⱼ the column mean when intercept is true and zero otherwise; the coefficients are on the scale of the data either way. Returns a LassoSolution whose σ is the sample standard deviation of the residuals.

The keyword arguments kwargs are fields of CDOptions that replace those of options, as in lasso(X, y, λ; maxIter=10_000, optTol=1e-8), and the random order of coordinate descent is drawn from rng.

X can be any matrix and y any vector. A Matrix, or a view of a range of its rows or of some of its columns, is read in place. A SparseMatrixCSC stays sparse; coordinate descent still reads its columns entry by entry, so for now it is slower than the dense matrix. Any other matrix, such as a transpose or a view of selected rows, is copied once into a Matrix (a SparseMatrixCSC if it is sparse). The same holds for every solver of this package.

With gram = true, coordinate descent works on the Gram matrix of the (centered) columns, A = X'X / n, instead of the residuals. Each coordinate step then costs O(p) instead of O(n), after O(np²) operations and p² numbers of memory to form A. The loss, the stopping rule and so the solution are the same, to the tolerance. Forming A pays off over the many passes of a path when n ≥ p, but rarely at one λ. The default, gram = :auto, uses it for LassoPath on a dense X with n ≥ p and p ≤ 2000, and never at one λ.

With observation weights w (nonnegative, with a positive sum) and an offset o, the loss is

∑ᵢ wᵢ (yᵢ - oᵢ - β₀ - Xᵢ'β)² / (2 ∑ᵢ wᵢ).

Dividing by ∑ᵢ wᵢ follows glmnet: λ means the same with and without weights, unit weights give the unweighted fit, integer weights the fit of replicated rows, and zero weights leave observations out. The intercept, the loadings of standardizeX (sqrt(∑ᵢ wᵢ (Xᵢⱼ - x̄ⱼ)² / ∑ᵢ wᵢ)) and the Gram matrix are weighted as well. The residuals are y - o - β₀ - Xβ, and with weights σ is their weighted root mean square, sqrt(∑ᵢ wᵢ rᵢ² / ∑ᵢ wᵢ).

Other penalties

alpha < 1 gives glmnet's elastic net, λ (α ∑ⱼ |βⱼ| + (1 - α) ‖β‖² / 2), for 0 < α ≤ 1. penalty gives another penalty of ProximalBase whose level λ replaces: NormL1(1, w) and NormL1Nonneg(1, w) (the lasso and the nonnegative lasso with weights w), ElasticNet(1, λ2, w) (λ ∑ⱼ wⱼ|βⱼ| + λ2 ‖β‖²/2, with λ2 fixed), Scaled(ElasticNet(α, 1 - α, w), 1) (the elastic net with weights on its ℓ1 part), and the nonconvex MCP(1, a) and SCAD(1, a), whose concavity a is kept. The level of the penalty given is not used: MCP(1, 3) means "MCP with a = 3".

alpha solves the objective of glmnet's documentation. glmnet's gaussian family scales y to unit standard deviation internally, which divides the ridge part by sd(y), the population standard deviation of y (weighted with weights). So glmnet's solutions are those of penalty = Scaled(ElasticNet(α, (1 - α) / sd(y)), 1), and they equal those of alpha when sd(y) = 1.

With standardizeX, the penalty applies to the coefficients of the standardized columns X[:, j] / sⱼ, sⱼ their standard deviations, as in glmnet and ncvreg; the coefficients returned are those of the columns of X. For the ℓ1 norm this is the same as the loadings ωⱼ = sⱼ, which is how it is computed. For the other penalties X is divided by s once (a copy), and s is stored as the solution's scales.

A nonconvex penalty has local solutions, and the one found depends on the starting point. As in ncvreg, it is computed along options.numSteps levels from λmax, the smallest level with a zero solution, down to λ, each solved to the tolerance and warm started from the previous one; numSteps = 0 solves at λ from zero. Use LassoPath for the solutions at many levels. For the steps of coordinate descent to be exact, MCP needs a > 1 and SCAD a > 2 on standardized columns. With options.warmStart false, a convex penalty other than the ℓ1 norm is also solved along such a path.

Bounds

lower and upper bound the coefficients, lower ≤ β ≤ upper, as glmnet's lower.limits and upper.limits do: each is a number or a vector with a bound per coefficient, and zero must be feasible, lower ≤ 0 ≤ upper. For example, lower = 0 gives the nonnegative lasso, and upper = 0 with lower = 0 for a coefficient keeps it at zero. The intercept is not bounded. The penalty becomes Constrained(g, IndBox(l, u)), which keeps coordinate descent separable, and λmax and screening use only the directions the bounds allow, so that λmax of the nonnegative lasso is maxⱼ (Xⱼ'y / n)₊ / ωⱼ. With standardizeX and a penalty other than the ℓ1 norm, which applies to s .* β, the bounds of the stored penalty are s .* lower and s .* upper. Bounds work with every penalty above, in lasso, LassoPath, adaptiveLasso (through its keywords), CVLassoPath and glmLasso.

CoordinateDescent.refitLassoPath — Method
refitLassoPath(path::LassoPath, X, y)

Least squares refits on the supports along a LassoPath. Returns a Dict that maps each distinct support S (a vector of column indices) to X[:, S] \ y, or, when the path has an intercept, to [1 X[:, S]] \ y, whose first entry is the intercept.

Consecutive supports along a path differ by a few columns, so the Cholesky factor of X[:, S]'X[:, S] is updated from one support to the next (ProximalSolvers' RefitCache) instead of computed again. A support with at least n columns, or one whose columns are nearly collinear, is refit by \, which gives the minimum-norm solution.

CoordinateDescent.scaledLasso! — Method
scaledLasso!(x, X, y, λ, ω, options=IterLassoOptions(); intercept=false, weights=nothing, offset=nothing,
             rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)
scaledLasso(X, y, λ, ω, options=IterLassoOptions(); intercept=false, weights=nothing, offset=nothing,
            rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)
scaledLasso!(x, f::CDSqrtQuadraticLoss, λ, ω, options=CDOptions(); rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)

Scaled lasso of Sun and Zhang (2012), which estimates the coefficients and the noise level jointly:

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2nσ) + σ/2 + λ ∑ⱼ ωⱼ |βⱼ|   over β and σ > 0.

The intercept β₀ is not penalized, and it is zero unless intercept is true. The minimum over σ is at σ̂ = ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖ / √n, so β̂ solves the square-root lasso of Belloni, Chernozhukov and Wang (2011),

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖ / √n + λ ∑ⱼ ωⱼ |βⱼ|,

which one run of coordinate descent solves (on CDSqrtLassoLoss, with the penalty level √n λ), with the options options.optionsCD; the other fields of IterLassoOptions are not used. Coordinate descent starts from and overwrites x (unless options.optionsCD.warmStart is false). Returns a LassoSolution whose σ is σ̂, and whose penalty is that of the lasso that β̂ solves, NormL1(λσ̂, ω).

The third form solves the same problem from sufficient statistics: with the loss CDSqrtQuadraticLoss(A, b, c), A = X'X/n, b = -X'y/n and c = y'y/n, it needs neither X nor y, and a coordinate update costs O(p) instead of O(n). With A = S, b = -S[:, j], c = S[j, j] and ω[j] = Inf it is the scaled lasso of variable j on the others from their covariance matrix S, as in the precision matrix estimator of Sun and Zhang (2013); for all the variables, check S once and build the losses with check = false. Its solution has no residuals (an empty vector) and no intercept.

The keyword arguments kwargs are fields of the options that replace those of options (of IterLassoOptions in the first two forms, of CDOptions in the third), and the random order of coordinate descent is drawn from rng. With observation weights w and an offset o, the loss is ∑ᵢ wᵢ rᵢ² / (2σ ∑ᵢ wᵢ) + σ/2 for the residuals r = y - o - β₀ - Xβ, so that σ̂ = sqrt(∑ᵢ wᵢ rᵢ² / ∑ᵢ wᵢ).

Before version 0.6.1, scaledLasso! alternated lasso fits with updates of σ̂ until the relative change of σ̂ was below options.optTol (by default 1e-2), which stopped short of the solution and solved a lasso at every iteration.

CoordinateDescent.sqrtLasso — Method
sqrtLasso(X, y, λ, options=CDOptions(); intercept=false, standardizeX=true, gram=:auto, weights=nothing,
          offset=nothing, rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)
sqrtLasso(X, y, λ, ω, options=CDOptions(); intercept=false, gram=:auto, weights=nothing, offset=nothing,
          rng=Random.default_rng(), kwargs...)

Solve the square-root lasso of Belloni, Chernozhukov and Wang (2011)

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖ + λ ∑ⱼ ωⱼ |βⱼ|

by coordinate descent, with an unpenalized intercept β₀ that is zero unless intercept is true. In the first form the loadings are the standard deviations ωⱼ = sqrt(∑ᵢ (Xᵢⱼ - x̄ⱼ)² / n) of the columns when standardizeX is true (centered only with an intercept) and ωⱼ = 1 otherwise. The optimal λ does not depend on the noise level. Returns a LassoSolution whose σ is the sample standard deviation of the residuals. The keyword arguments kwargs, gram and rng are as for lasso; on the Gram matrix the loss is the square root of a quadratic (as in CDSqrtQuadraticLoss). With observation weights w and an offset o, the loss is the weighted norm sqrt(∑ᵢ wᵢ (yᵢ - oᵢ - β₀ - Xᵢ'β)²), the loadings are weighted as in lasso, and σ is the weighted root mean square of the residuals.

CoordinateDescent.adaptiveLasso — Method
adaptiveLasso(X, y, λ, loss::ProximalBase.ScalarLoss, options=CDOptions(); init=:lasso, initλ=λ, γ=1,
              intercept=true, standardizeX=true, kwargs...)

The adaptive lasso of a generalized linear model: as above, with the loss of glmLasso (ProximalBase.Logistic(), ProximalBase.Poisson(), ...) in place of least squares. The first estimate β̃ is the glmLasso fit at initλ (init = :lasso), the ridge-penalized fit ∑ᵢ wᵢ ℓ(β₀ + xᵢ'β + oᵢ, yᵢ) / ∑ᵢ wᵢ + initλ ‖β‖² / 2 on the standardized columns with standardizeX (init = :ridge, glmnet's alpha = 0), or a given vector; the second fit is glmLasso with the loadings ωⱼ = 1/|β̃ⱼ|^γ and no standardization. The keywords kwargs (weights, offset, lower, upper, newtonTol, newtonMaxIter) apply to both fits. Returns the GLMLassoSolution of the second fit.

CoordinateDescent.adaptiveLasso — Method
adaptiveLasso(X, y, λ, options=CDOptions(); init=:lasso, initλ=λ, γ=1, intercept=false, standardizeX=false,
              kwargs...)

The adaptive lasso of Zou (2006),

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + λ ∑ⱼ |βⱼ| / |β̃ⱼ|^γ,

whose loadings come from a first estimate β̃: large coefficients are penalized less, so that they are less biased, and small ones more, so that fewer variables that do not matter are selected. β̃ is

  • init = :lasso: the lasso at initλ, by default λ, with intercept, standardizeX and the keywords kwargs;
  • init = :ridge: ridge regression at initλ, ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + initλ ‖β‖² / 2, on the standardized columns with standardizeX (glmnet's alpha = 0);
  • init = β̃, a vector: for example least squares, X \ y when n > p, or the coefficients of a lasso whose level was chosen by cross-validation.

A coefficient with β̃ⱼ = 0 has an infinite loading and stays at zero. The second fit has no standardization, as with γ = 1 the loadings already make it invariant to the scales of the columns. The keywords kwargs (weights, offset, gram, rng and the fields of CDOptions) apply to both fits. Returns the LassoSolution of the second fit, whose penalty is NormL1(λ, ω) with the loadings ωⱼ = 1/|β̃ⱼ|^γ.

Reference: H. Zou (2006). The adaptive lasso and its oracle properties. Journal of the American Statistical Association 101(476), 1418–1429.

CoordinateDescent.GLMLassoPath — Type
GLMLassoPath(X, y, loss = ProximalBase.Logistic(), options = CDOptions(); nλ = 100, λminratio, kwargs...)
GLMLassoPath(X, y, λpath, loss = ProximalBase.Logistic(), options = CDOptions();
             intercept = true, standardizeX = true, weights = nothing, offset = nothing,
             alpha = 1, penalty = nothing, lower = -Inf, upper = Inf, screening = true, max_hat_s = Inf,
             devmax = 0.999, newtonTol = 1e-8, newtonMaxIter = 100)

Solutions β0path[k] and βpath[k] of the penalized generalized linear model of glmLasso along a decreasing sequence λpath, each one warm started from the previous solution and its intercept, by proximal Newton with the inner coordinate descent of options (ProximalSolvers' solve_path with ProxNewton).

Without λpath, the path has nλ values spaced evenly on the log scale from λmax, the smallest value whose solution is zero (at the intercept of the fit with β = 0), down to λminratio ⋅ λmax; λminratio defaults to 0.01 when X has more columns than rows and to 1e-4 otherwise. The first value is λmax times 1 + √eps, so that rounding cannot make the first solution nonzero.

With screening, each λ is solved first over the coordinates kept by the sequential strong rule of Tibshirani et al. (2012), and the optimality conditions of the others are checked afterwards (kktChecks[k] times at λpath[k]), as in LassoPath. The path stops early after the first solution with more than max_hat_s nonzero coefficients, or once the fit explains a fraction devmax of the null deviance, as glmnet's does: along a logistic path on separable data the coefficients grow without bound, and the explained fraction devratio[k] = 1 - deviance / nulldeviance tends to one. devmax = 1 turns this off. The returned λpath then contains only the values that were solved for. iterations[k] and converged[k] are the passes of coordinate descent at λpath[k], summed over the proximal Newton steps, and whether the solve met its stopping rules. nobs is the number of observations.

alpha, penalty, lower and upper are as for glmLasso; along a path of a nonconvex penalty, each solution is the local one reached from the previous solution. penalty holds the penalty at level one (NormL1(1, ω) for the lasso), applied to scales .* β when scales is not nothing. coef, predict and nobs apply to the path, as for LassoPath, and fit(GLMLassoPath, X, y, ...) is GLMLassoPath(X, y, ...).

CoordinateDescent.GLMLassoSolution — Type
GLMLassoSolution

Result returned by glmLasso.

Fields

  • x::SparseIterate: the estimated coefficients β
  • intercept: the unpenalized intercept β₀, zero unless the solver was called with intercept = true
  • penalty: the penalty at which x was computed: NormL1(λ, ω), the penalty of alpha or penalty at the level λ, or either on the box of lower and upper, Constrained(g, IndBox(l, u))

  • deviance: 2 ∑ᵢ wᵢ (ℓ(ηᵢ, yᵢ) - minᵤ ℓ(u, yᵢ)), twice the negative log-likelihood ratio against the saturated model for the logistic and Poisson losses, with the observation weights wᵢ

  • converged::Bool: whether proximal Newton, and coordinate descent at each of its steps, met their stopping rules
  • hasintercept::Bool: whether the solver fitted an intercept
  • scales: nothing when penalty applies to x, or the scales s of the columns when it applies to s .* x (standardizeX with a penalty other than the ℓ1 norm), as for LassoSolution

The functions of StatsAPI apply to it: coef, predict (on the scale of the response or of the linear predictor), fitted, nobs, dof, deviance and nulldeviance.

CoordinateDescent.glmLasso — Method
glmLasso(X, y, λ, loss = ProximalBase.Logistic(), options = CDOptions();
         intercept = true, standardizeX = true, weights = nothing, offset = nothing,
         alpha = 1, penalty = nothing, lower = -Inf, upper = Inf, newtonTol = 1e-8, newtonMaxIter = 100)

Solve the penalized generalized linear model

minimize  ∑ᵢ wᵢ ℓ(β₀ + xᵢ'β + oᵢ, yᵢ) / ∑ᵢ wᵢ + λ ∑ⱼ ωⱼ |βⱼ|

for a scalar loss ℓ of ProximalBase: ProximalBase.Logistic(), the negative log-likelihood of logistic regression (yᵢ ∈ [0, 1], usually {0, 1}); ProximalBase.Poisson(), Poisson regression with the log link (yᵢ ≥ 0); ProximalBase.Huber(δ); or a loss of your own. The observation weights wᵢ (nonnegative, one when omitted) and the offset oᵢ (zero when omitted) are those of weights and offset. The intercept β₀ is not penalized, and it is zero unless intercept is true. With standardizeX the loadings ωⱼ are the standard deviations of the columns, weighted by w and centered at their weighted means with an intercept, so that the coefficients are on the scale of the data; otherwise ωⱼ = 1. These are glmnet's conventions and defaults. lower and upper bound the coefficients, lower ≤ β ≤ upper, as for lasso.

alpha and penalty give the elastic net and the other penalties, as for lasso: alpha < 1 is glmnet's elastic net λ (α ∑ⱼ |βⱼ| + (1 - α) ‖β‖² / 2), and penalty a penalty of ProximalBase whose level λ replaces (ElasticNet(1, λ2), MCP(1, a), SCAD(1, a), NormL1Nonneg(1), or NormL1(1, w) for weights). With standardizeX, such a penalty applies to the coefficients of the standardized columns, as in glmnet; the coefficients returned are those of the columns of X, and the scales are stored in the solution's scales. The elastic net equals glmnet's binomial and poisson fits. A nonconvex penalty (MCP, SCAD) is computed along options.numSteps levels from λmax whatever options.warmStart, as ncvreg does; numSteps = 0 solves at λ from zero. The solutions are stationary points of the objective above. ncvreg's binomial and poisson families do not solve it: their coordinate steps scale the concavity of the penalty by the weighted curvature of each column at the current fit (they divide by v (1 - 1/a) where the exact step divides by v - 1/a), so their solutions differ (in its gaussian family v = 1 and the two agree).

The problem is solved by proximal Newton (ProximalSolvers' ProxNewton, the method of glmnet): each step minimizes the weighted least-squares model of the loss at the current fit by coordinate descent with options, then backtracks, until the objective changes by less than newtonTol relative to its value or after newtonMaxIter steps. With options.warmStart false (or a nonconvex penalty), the solution is computed along options.numSteps levels from λmax (see GLMLassoPath), each warm started from the previous one; otherwise it is computed directly, starting from β = 0 and the intercept of the fit with β = 0. Returns a GLMLassoSolution.

sol = glmLasso(X, y, 0.05)                               # logistic
sol = glmLasso(X, counts, 0.05, ProximalBase.Poisson(); offset = log.(exposure))

CoordinateDescent.groupLasso — Method
groupLasso(X, y, λ, groups, options = CDOptions(); intercept = true, standardize = :orthonormal,
           groupweights = sqrt.(length.(groups)))

The group lasso

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + λ ∑ₖ wₖ ‖X̃_Gₖ βGₖ‖₂ / √n

for disjoint groups Gₖ that cover the columns of X (give a column its own group to treat it alone, and a zero weight to leave a group unpenalized), with an unpenalized intercept β₀ that is zero unless intercept is true, and X̃_G the columns of G, centered with an intercept. The weights default to wₖ = √|Gₖ|.

With standardize = :orthonormal, the default, the penalty is on the fitted values of each group, as above, which makes the estimate invariant to a change of basis within a group (Simon and Tibshirani, 2012; R's grpreg). It is fitted on orthonormalized groups: each X̃_G is factored by a pivoted QR as Z_G R̃, with Z_G'Z_G / n = I, the group lasso is solved in the coefficients θ_G = R̃ β_G by block coordinate descent, whose steps are then exact, and mapped back. The columns of a group that are linear combinations of its other columns get zero coefficients. With standardize = :none, the penalty is λ ∑ₖ wₖ ‖βGₖ‖₂ on the columns as given.

Coordinate descent runs with options; with options.warmStart false the solution is computed along options.numSteps levels from λmax. Returns a GroupLassoSolution. See also GroupLassoPath.

CoordinateDescent.multiLasso — Method
multiLasso(X, Y, λ, options = CDOptions(); intercept = true, standardizeX = true)

The multi-response lasso of the n × q responses Y on X,

minimize  ‖Y - 1β₀' - XB‖²_F / (2n) + λ ∑ₖ ωₖ ‖B[k, :]‖₂

which selects the same predictors for all the responses (glmnet's mgaussian family). The intercepts β₀ are not penalized, and they are zero unless intercept is true. CoordinateDescent.CVLassoPath — Method
CVLassoPath(X, y, options = CDOptions(); nfolds = 10, folds = nothing, rng = Random.default_rng(),
            threads = true, nλ = 100, λminratio, intercept = false, standardizeX = true, kwargs...)

K-fold cross-validation of the lasso path of LassoPath (with the same options and keywords): the path on all the data gives the levels, each fold's path is computed on the other folds at those levels, and the mean squared error on the held-out fold estimates the prediction error at each level. Standardization and the intercept are computed on each training fold, so nothing leaks from the held-out one.

cvm is the average of the fold errors weighted by the fold sizes, and cvse its standard error, sqrt(∑ₖ nₖ (eₖ - cvm)² / n / (K - 1)), as in glmnet. λmin minimizes cvm, and λ1se is the largest level whose cvm is within cvse of the minimum (the one-standard-error rule). A fold path that stops early (max_hat_s) leaves the remaining levels out of the averages.

The folds are a balanced random assignment drawn from rng, or the vector folds of fold labels 1, …, K given, for example grouped or blocked folds for dependent data. With threads, the folds run as tasks on the available threads; each fold draws its random coordinate orders from a generator seeded from rng, so that the result does not depend on the number of threads.

Returns a CVLassoPath; coef(cv) and predict(cv, X) use the solution at λ1se, or at λmin with λ = :λmin.

CoordinateDescent.ebic — Method
ebic(path::LassoPath, X, y; γ = 0.5)
aic(path::LassoPath, X, y)
bic(path::LassoPath, X, y)

Information criteria along the lasso path fitted to X and y, one per level of path.λpath:

  • aic = n log(RSS/n) + 2 df;
  • bic = n log(RSS/n) + log(n) df;
  • ebic = bic + 2γ log C(p, s), the extended BIC of Chen and Chen (2008) for large model spaces, with s nonzero coefficients among p.

RSS is the residual sum of squares and df the number of nonzero coefficients plus one for an intercept, an unbiased estimate of the degrees of freedom of the lasso (Zou, Hastie and Tibshirani, 2007). A level with df ≥ n gets NaN. The level that minimizes a criterion c is path.λpath[argmin(c)], with the NaNs left out.

CoordinateDescent.doubleSelection — Method
doubleSelection(y, d, X; method = :doubleselection, level = 0.95, forced = Int[], post = true, kwargs...)

Inference on the effect α of a treatment d in y = αd + Xβ + ε with many controls X, after selecting them by rlasso (kwargs go to it), as R's hdm rlassoEffect computes it:

  • method = :doubleselection (Belloni, Chernozhukov and Hansen, 2014): select the controls that predict y and those that predict d, then regress y on d, the union of both (and the forced controls) and an intercept. The standard error is the robust one of hdm, √(mean(v² ξ²) / (n mean(v²)²)), with ξ the residuals of that regression scaled by √(n/(n - s - 1)) for s selected controls, and v the residuals of d on the selected controls and an intercept.
  • method = :partialling: regress the residuals of rlasso of y on X on those of d on X, with an intercept; the standard error is that of least squares, as in hdm.

The confidence interval is estimate ± z se with the normal quantile z at level. Returns a TreatmentEffect.

CoordinateDescent.rlasso — Method
rlasso(X, y, options = CDOptions(); post = true, intercept = true, c = post ? 1.1 : 0.5,
       γ = 0.1 / log(n), maxIter = 15, tol = 1e-5)

The lasso with data-driven penalty loadings as R's hdm computes it (rlasso with its defaults: heteroskedastic and X-independent penalty), the procedure of Belloni, Chen, Chernozhukov and Hansen (2012):

  1. Start from the residuals of least squares on the five columns most correlated with y, and compute the loadings Γⱼ from them (see feasibleLasso!).
  2. Solve the lasso ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + λ0 ∑ⱼ Γⱼ |βⱼ| with λ0 = theoryLambda(n, p; c, γ), at half the level in the first step when post is true, as hdm does.
  3. With post, refit least squares on the selected columns; update the loadings from the residuals; repeat 2–3 until the standard deviation of the residuals changes by less than tol, or maxIter times.

The coefficients returned are the refitted ones when post is true. With intercept, the columns and y are centered and β₀ = ȳ - x̄'β. Each lasso is solved by coordinate descent with options, warm started from the previous one. Returns an RLassoSolution. See also postLasso and doubleSelection.

CoordinateDescent.theoryLambda — Method
theoryLambda(n, p; c = 1.1, γ = 0.1 / log(n))

The penalty level λ0 = c Φ⁻¹(1 - γ/(2p)) / √n of Belloni, Chen, Chernozhukov and Hansen (2012) for the lasso with data-driven loadings,

minimize  ‖y - β₀ - Xβ‖² / (2n) + λ0 ∑ⱼ Γⱼ |βⱼ|,   Γⱼ = sqrt(∑ᵢ (Xᵢⱼ - x̄ⱼ)² εᵢ² / n),

the scale of feasibleLasso!. It is R's hdm penalty 2c √n Φ⁻¹(1 - γ/(2p)) for ‖y - Xβ‖² + λ ∑ⱼ Γⱼ |βⱼ|, divided by 2n, with hdm's defaults for c and γ. Φ⁻¹ is the standard normal quantile.
