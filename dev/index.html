HighDimensionalModels.jl

Lasso estimators computed by coordinate descent. For a response $y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $n$ observations of $p$ predictors in the rows of $X$, the lasso is

\[\underset{\beta_0,\,\beta}{\text{minimize}} \quad \frac{1}{2n}\,\|y - \beta_0 - X\beta\|^2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^p \omega_j |\beta_j|,\]

with an unpenalized intercept $\beta_0$ and penalty loadings $\omega_j \ge 0$. HighDimensionalModels solves it at one penalty level $\lambda$ and along a path of levels, and solves the estimators built on it:

  • the lasso, with an intercept, standardized columns, penalty loadings, observation weights and offsets;
  • the square-root and the scaled lasso, whose $\lambda$ does not depend on the noise level, and the heteroscedasticity-robust (feasible) lasso;
  • lasso paths, with screening, refitting and the functions of StatsAPI;
  • the group lasso;
  • varying-coefficient models by local polynomial regression, with the kernels of KernelSmoothing.jl, and sparse ones with a group lasso penalty.

The coordinate descent itself (the passes over the coordinates and the active set, the penalty paths with screening, the reweighting of the scaled and feasible lasso) is that of ProximalSolvers.jl, on the losses and penalties of ProximalBase.jl. coordinate_descent! runs it on other losses and on any penalty that separates across coordinates, including your own.

Installation

Before the first registration, use a checkout with registered dependencies:

git clone --branch master https://github.com/mlakolar/HighDimensionalModels.jl.git
cd HighDimensionalModels.jl
julia --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=.

HighDimensionalModels requires Julia 1.10 or later.

A first example

The lasso with an intercept at $\lambda = 0.1$, on 200 observations of 50 predictors of which the first five matter:

using HighDimensionalModels, Random

rng = Xoshiro(1)
n, p = 200, 50
X = randn(rng, n, p)
y = 1.0 .+ X[:, 1:5] * ones(5) + randn(rng, n)

sol = lasso(X, y, 0.1; intercept = true)

The family

HighDimensionalModels is one of five Julia packages for high-dimensional statistics, three for estimation and inference and two for the optimization beneath them:

Package
HighDimensionalModels.jl (this package)the lasso and its paths; the group, square-root, scaled and heteroscedastic lasso; varying-coefficient models
CovarianceSelection.jlGaussian graphical models: the graphical lasso, CLIME and ACLIME, precision matrices by the scaled lasso, the fused graphical lasso, and the difference of two precision matrices
DiffPrecTest.jlconfidence intervals and tests for the difference of two precision matrices
ProximalSolvers.jlthe solvers: coordinate descent with penalty paths, screening and active sets; proximal gradient methods, proximal Newton and ADMM
ProximalBase.jlthe building blocks: losses, penalties and constraints with their gradients and proximal operators, and sparse iterates; documentation with the mathematics

Most users need one of the first three. To solve a problem of your own, use ProximalSolvers; to write your own algorithm, ProximalBase.

Development tests

From the repository root, run julia scripts/test_registered.jl for the full suite with registered dependencies on Julia 1.10 and newer. In a separate checkout, julia scripts/test_master.jl checks integration with dependency master checkouts. The repository's CONTRIBUTING.md describes the naming convention and bootstrap scripts.
