Varying-coefficient models

In a varying-coefficient model the coefficients change with an index variable $z$, such as time or age:

\[y_i = \sum_{j=1}^p X_{ij}\, \beta_j(z_i) + \varepsilon_i.\]

A column of ones in $X$ gives a varying intercept.

Local polynomial fits

varying_coefficient(X, z, y, zgrid; kernel, bandwidth, degree) estimates the coefficient functions at each point $z_0$ of zgrid by local polynomial regression. Near $z_0$ each $\beta_j$ is a polynomial of degree $q$ in $z - z_0$, and the polynomials are fitted by weighted least squares,

\[\underset{\theta}{\text{minimize}} \quad \sum_{i=1}^n w_i K_h(z_i - z_0)\Big(y_i - \sum_{j=1}^p X_{ij}\sum_{r=0}^q \theta_{jr}(z_i - z_0)^r\Big)^2, \qquad K_h(d) = k(d/h)/h,\]

with observation weights $w_i$. Then $r!\,\theta_{jr}$ estimates the derivative $\beta_j^{(r)}(z_0)$: coef(fit) returns the estimates of $\beta_j(z_0)$, and coef(fit; derivative = r) those of the derivatives.

The kernel $k$ is a function of the scaled distance $u = (z_i - z_0)/h$: the kernels of KernelSmoothing.jl (EpanechnikovKernel(), GaussianKernel(), whose bandwidth is a standard deviation, UniformKernel(), and others), or any function of $u$. With a compactly supported kernel only the observations within $h$ of $z_0$ enter; when they do not determine the polynomials, the fit of minimum norm is taken and the rank of the fit records it.

With se = true, the fit also gives the fitted values and residuals of the local fits at the observations (one more local fit per observation), and heteroskedasticity-robust (sandwich) standard errors, stderror(fit) and confint(fit). These describe the variance of the local estimate, not its bias, so an interval covers $\beta_j(z_0)$ when the bandwidth is small enough for the bias to be negligible. predict(fit, X, z) interpolates the estimates linearly between the points of the grid.

varying_coefficient_cv chooses the bandwidth by leave-one-out cross-validation. The local fit is linear in $y$, so the prediction of $y_i$ without observation $i$ comes from the fit with it, $(\hat y_i - H_{ii} y_i)/(1 - H_{ii})$, at the cost of one local fit per observation.

Selecting the variables

varying_coefficient_lasso(X, z, y, zgrid, λ; kernel, bandwidth) adds a penalty that selects, at each point, the variables whose coefficient functions are not zero nearby:

\[\underset{\theta}{\text{minimize}} \quad \frac{\sum_i v_i\big(y_i - \sum_j X_{ij}\sum_r \theta_{jr}((z_i - z_0)/h)^r\big)^2}{2\sum_i v_i} + \lambda\, P(\theta), \qquad v_i = w_i K_h(z_i - z_0).\]

The loss is the mean squared error on the local sample, so $\lambda$ is on the scale of an ordinary lasso. With penalty = :group, the default, $P$ is the group lasso over the $q + 1$ coefficients of each variable, on the fitted values of its locally weighted columns (the orthonormalized groups of group_lasso), so that a variable enters with all its polynomial terms. With penalty = :lasso, $P$ is the ℓ1 norm with the loadings of the standardized local columns. With refit, the default, the selected variables are refitted at each point by the unpenalized local fit, which removes the shrinkage. varying_coefficient_lasso_cv chooses the bandwidth by leave-one-out cross-validation, one local lasso per observation and bandwidth.

The example on varying-coefficient models goes through these functions.
