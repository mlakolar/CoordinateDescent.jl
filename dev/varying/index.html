Varying-coefficient models

In a varying-coefficient model the coefficients change with an index variable $z$, such as time or age:

\[y_i = \sum_{j=1}^p X_{ij}\, \beta_j(z_i) + \varepsilon_i.\]

locpoly estimates $\beta(z_0)$ at a point $z_0$ by local polynomial regression. Each $\beta_j(z)$ is approximated by a polynomial of degree $q$ in $z - z_0$, which expands the design to $\tilde X_i = X_i \otimes (1, z_i - z_0, \dots, (z_i - z_0)^q)$, and the observations are weighted by a kernel $K_h(z_i, z_0)$ with bandwidth $h$, a GaussianKernel or an EpanechnikovKernel. The coefficients of variable $j$ are its value and its derivatives at $z_0$, up to factorials.

locpolyl1 also selects the variables. At each point $z_0$ of a grid it solves the weighted lasso

\[\underset{\beta}{\text{minimize}} \quad \frac{1}{2n} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i (y_i - \tilde X_i^\top \beta)^2 + \lambda_0 \sum_k s_k |\beta_k|, \qquad w_i = K_h(z_i, z_0),\]

with loadings $s_k = \sqrt{\sum_i w_i \tilde X_{ik}^2 / n}$ that standardize the expanded columns, warm started from the solution at the previous point, and it can refit weighted least squares on the selected variables.

The bandwidth can be chosen by leave-one-out cross-validation, CoordinateDescent.lvocv_locpoly and CoordinateDescent.lvocv_locpolyl1, or on a validation set, CoordinateDescent.split_locpoly. CoordinateDescent.getStandardError and CoordinateDescent.getStandardErrorHEW give standard errors of the estimates. The example on varying-coefficient models goes through them.
