Joint-sparse transfer multitask learning

transfer_multitask and debias fit TMTL(Fused) and TMTL(Debiased) of Lim and Kolar (2026), which borrow strength from source domains to estimate a row-sparse coefficient matrix in a target domain. TransferMultitaskPath computes warm-started paths and CVTransferMultitaskPath tunes both stages on target data only.

The model

Domain $\ell=0$ is the target and $\ell=1,\dots,L$ are the sources. Domain $\ell$ has $n_\ell$ observations of the same $K$ responses on the same $p$ predictors, with one design $X^\ell$ for all responses or one design $X^{\ell k}$ per response (Appendix B of the paper). Sizes may differ across domains; the responses of a domain share its rows. With $N=\sum_{\ell=0}^L n_\ell$ over all domains, the target included, TMTL(Fused) solves

\[\min_{B^0,\dots,B^L,\ \beta_0}\; \frac{1}{2NK}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \|y^{\ell k}-\beta_0^{\ell k}-X^{\ell k}B^\ell_{\cdot k}\|^2 +\lambda_0\|B^0\|_{2,1} +\lambda_0\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}a_\ell\|B^\ell-B^0\|_{2,1},\]

where $\|A\|_{2,1}=\sum_j\|A_{j\cdot}\|_2$ sums the row norms. The target block is row sparse, the contrasts $B^\ell-B^0$ are row sparse, and the source blocks need not be. Each observation has the same weight in the loss. The intercepts $\beta_0^{\ell k}$ are unpenalized, one per domain and response, so domains may have different means; with intercept = false there are none.

The contrast weights are contrast_weights = :sqrt, $a_\ell=8\sqrt{n_\ell/N}$ (the default), :linear, $a_\ell=8n_\ell/N$, or a vector of positive values. A larger $a_\ell$ penalizes the contrast with source $\ell$ more and so borrows more from it. The designs are not standardized: the contrasts compare coefficients across domains, so the predictors need a common scale in every domain, which per-domain standardization would change. Centering within each domain and response only removes intercepts.

The reported estimate is the sample-size weighted average of all fitted blocks,

\[\widehat W^F=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\frac{n_\ell}{N}\widehat B^\ell,\]

not $\widehat B^0$. $\widehat W^F$ is generally not row sparse, since the source blocks are not. Target predictions use the intercepts $\bar y^0-\bar x^{0\prime}W$ of whichever slope matrix $W$ is reported.

The correction

TMTL(Debiased) corrects the fused estimate on the target data,

\[\widehat D=\arg\min_{D,\ d_0}\; \frac{1}{2n_0K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|y^{0k}-d_0^k-X^{0k}(\widehat W^F_{\cdot k}+D_{\cdot k})\|^2 +\widetilde\lambda\|D\|_{2,1},\qquad \widehat W^D=\widehat W^F+\widehat D.\]

It is a group lasso of the target residuals $Y^0-X^0\widehat W^F$ on the unstandardized target design. For a shared design this is multi_lasso(X⁰, Y⁰ - X⁰Ŵᶠ, K * λ̃; standardize_x = false); note the factor $K$, since multi_lasso divides its loss by $2n$, not $2nK$. The correction uses the same target sample as the fused fit, so debias takes the target data again: debias(fit, (X⁰, Y⁰), λ̃). The correction is zero when

\[\widetilde\lambda\ge\widetilde\lambda_{\max} =\max_j\Bigl\|\tfrac{1}{n_0K}\bigl[\widetilde X^{0k\prime}_{\cdot j} \bigl(\widetilde y^{0k}-\widetilde X^{0k}\widehat W^F_{\cdot k}\bigr)\bigr]_{k}\Bigr\|_2,\]

with tildes for centered columns. "Debiased" refers to the aggregation bias of $\widehat W^F$ in point estimation, which the correction reduces; it is not an inferential (desparsified) estimator, and the fits give no confidence intervals or tests. The correction is computed by the row solver of multitask_lasso on unstandardized columns, which reports its own relative duality gap, so the fused and correction diagnostics are separate fields of the result.

Coordinate descent

The default solver works in the coordinates of the paper's Appendix C.1,

\[\theta^0=B^0,\qquad\theta^\ell=B^\ell-B^0\quad(\ell\ge1),\qquad B^\ell=\theta^0+\theta^\ell,\qquad \widehat W^F=\theta^0+\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{n_\ell}{N}\theta^\ell,\]

where the penalty $\lambda_0\|\theta^0\|_{2,1}+\sum_\ell\lambda_0a_\ell\|\theta^\ell\|_{2,1}$ separates over components and rows. Column $k$ of $\theta^0$ enters response $k$ of every domain, and column $k$ of $\theta^\ell$ only that of domain $\ell$. Block coordinate descent updates one row of one component at a time. With centered columns $\widetilde x^{\ell k}_j$, residuals $r^{\ell k}$ and scores $S^\ell_{jk}=\langle\widetilde x^{\ell k}_j,r^{\ell k}\rangle/(NK)$, the gradient of row $j$ of $\theta^0$ is $-\sum_\ell S^\ell_{j\cdot}$ and that of $\theta^\ell$ is $-S^\ell_{j\cdot}$. The coordinate curvatures are $h^0_{jk}=\sum_\ell\|\widetilde x^{\ell k}_j\|^2/(NK)$ and $h^\ell_{jk}=\|\widetilde x^{\ell k}_j\|^2/(NK)$. The row step is a group soft threshold at the curvature $H=\max_kh_{jk}$, exact for a shared design and a majorization with task-specific designs. An update changes the residuals of every domain the component enters.

A column that is constant in one domain does not force its coefficients to zero: a row of $\theta^0$ is determined by the domains where the column varies. Only coefficients that enter no loss stay at zero. The passes, the change rule and its relative scale follow the row solver of multitask_lasso, with CDOptions; continuation is used when warm_start = false.

Certificates and λmax

The fused fit is certified by one global relative duality gap. Let

\[M=\max\Bigl\{\max_j\Bigl\|\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}S^\ell_{j\cdot}\Bigr\|_2,\; \max_{\ell\ge1,\,j}\frac{\|S^\ell_{j\cdot}\|_2}{a_\ell}\Bigr\}.\]

The dual point is $u=t\,r/(NK)$ with $t=\min(1,\lambda_0/M)$, and its value $D(u)=\sum\langle\widetilde y,u\rangle-\tfrac{NK}{2}\sum\|u\|^2$. The gap is the whole primal objective minus $D(u)$, divided by the objective, with the guards of the row solver; it is unavailable (NaN) at $\lambda_0=0$ and at a zero objective. objective and gap are recomputed from the returned blocks and intercepts. A positive gaptol in the options requires the gap; paths require the path target.

At zero coefficients, $M$ is $\lambda_{\max}$, the smallest level at which every block is zero, not only $\widehat W^F$. The automatic grid of TransferMultitaskPath starts at $\lambda_{\max}(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{eps}})$. Screening applies the sequential strong rule to each component: row $j$ of component $c$ is kept when it is active or $\|g_{cj}\|_2\ge w_c(2\lambda_k-\lambda_{k-1})$, with $w_0=1$ and $w_\ell=a_\ell$. After convergence every excluded row is checked against $\|g_{cj}\|_2\le w_c\lambda_k$ and violations are added back (kkt_checks counts the rounds).

ADMM

solver = :admm is the paper's two-block ADMM (Appendix A), kept as an optional backend for fidelity to the paper and as an independent check of coordinate descent. The copies $\Gamma^{0\ell}=B^0$ ($\ell=0,\dots,L$) and $\Gamma^\ell=B^\ell$ ($\ell\ge1$) carry the penalties; each constraint has its own penalty $\rho$ and scaled dual. Each iteration

  • updates $B^0$ and each $B^\ell$ by a ridge-type least-squares solve; the thin SVD of each centered design is cached, so every penalty value and $p>n_\ell$ reuse it;
  • soft-thresholds the rows of $B^0+U^0$ for $\Gamma^{00}$, and updates each pair $(\Gamma^{0\ell},\Gamma^\ell)$ exactly by eq. (37): with $\mu_\ell=\rho_{0\ell}\rho_\ell/(\rho_{0\ell}+\rho_\ell)$ and $r=S_{\lambda_0a_\ell/\mu_\ell}(q^\ell-q^{0\ell})$, $\Gamma^{0\ell}=(\rho_{0\ell}q^{0\ell}+\rho_\ell q^\ell-\rho_\ell r)/(\rho_{0\ell}+\rho_\ell)$ and $\Gamma^\ell=\Gamma^{0\ell}+r$;
  • updates the scaled duals and computes the primal residual $B-\Gamma$ and dual residual $\rho(\Gamma_{\mathrm{old}}-\Gamma)$ of each constraint.

It stops when every constraint has primal residual at most $\sqrt{pK}\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}\max(\|B\|,\|\Gamma\|)$ and dual residual at most $\sqrt{pK}\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}\rho\|U\|$. During the first adapt iterations the penalties are balanced: $\rho$ is multiplied by increase (up to rho_max) when the primal residual exceeds balance times the dual residual, and divided by decrease in the opposite case; the scaled dual is multiplied by $\rho_{\mathrm{old}}/\rho_{\mathrm{new}}$. The penalties are then fixed, so the standard two-block convergence argument applies. The settings are given as a named tuple, for example admm = (; abstol = 1e-10, reltol = 1e-10, max_iter = 20_000); the defaults are listed in transfer_multitask. The result reports the last residuals and the final penalties. ADMM returns $\theta^0=\Gamma^{00}$ and the contrasts $\Gamma^\ell-\Gamma^{0\ell}$, so zero rows are exact; the objective and the gap are computed at that point as for coordinate descent.

Paths and cross-validation

TransferMultitaskPath solves a decreasing sequence of $\lambda_0$ with fixed contrast weights, warm starting each level. coef(path) and predict(path, X) give a result per level.

CVTransferMultitaskPath tunes the weight family, $\lambda_0$ and then $\widetilde\lambda$ by held-out target prediction:

  • Folds are over target rows, the same for every response, family, level and correction level. Every source row is in every training fit.
  • For each fold and family, the fused path is fitted on the target training rows and all sources at the full-data grid of that family. Sizes, $N$, the :sqrt and :linear weights and the centering are recomputed from that training data, and the strong rule restarts.
  • A fold's score is its target sum of squared errors divided by its held-out rows times $K$; cvm averages the folds weighted by their sizes. The family and $\lambda_0$ minimize cvm among candidates converged on the full data and in every fold.
  • $\widetilde\lambda$ is then chosen with the same folds. Each fold corrects its own fused fit at the selected family and $\lambda_0$ on its training residuals; the full-data fused fit is never used inside a fold.
  • The selected settings are fitted on all the data: fused is the full-data fused fit and solution adds the full-data correction.

The usual limits apply. The grids come from the full data, and the folds are reused for the sequential selection, so cvm and cvm_correction are not unbiased estimates of the selected fit's error; independent test data are needed for that. The selection compares target prediction only, and nothing guarantees that transfer improves on a target-only fit: when the sources differ more than the target sample can detect, borrowing can hurt (negative transfer).

Results

A TransferMultitaskSolution holds the reported target slopes W and intercepts intercept, the fused estimate fused, the correction correction (or nothing), the domain blocks B, the contrasts and the domain intercepts. coef(sol) gives the target coefficients with the intercepts in the first row; predict(sol, X⁰new) gives target predictions; with domain = ℓ both use $\widehat B^\ell$ and its intercepts instead (domain = 0 gives $\widehat B^0$, not $W$). Task-specific designs are passed to predict as a vector of $K$ matrices.

Not included

Three extensions of the paper are not implemented: the target-certified projection of Theorem 4 and Appendix E, which needs explicit calibration inputs; inference for these estimators; and responses missing within a domain. See examples/17_transfer_multitask.jl for one draw comparing TMTL(Fused), TMTL(Debiased), a target-only fit and a pooled fit with domain intercepts.
