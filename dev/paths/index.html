Paths, screening and solver options

Lasso paths

LassoPath(X, y) solves the lasso along a decreasing sequence of penalty levels, each warm started from the previous solution. By default the sequence has nλ = 100 levels spaced evenly on the log scale from $\lambda_{\max}$, the smallest level whose solution is zero, down to λminratio times $\lambda_{\max}$, with λminratio 0.01 when $p > n$ and $10^{-4}$ otherwise; LassoPath(X, y, λpath) takes levels of your own. Unlike lasso, a path standardizes the columns by default (standardize_x = true).

  • Screening. Each level $\lambda_k$ is solved first over the coordinates kept by the sequential strong rule of Tibshirani et al. (2012), those with $|X_j^\top r(\lambda_{k-1})| / n \ge (2\lambda_k - \lambda_{k-1})\,\omega_j$ for the residuals $r(\lambda_{k-1})$ at the previous level. The optimality conditions of the other coordinates are checked afterwards; those that violate them are added, and the level is solved again. kkt_checks[k] counts the checks at level $k$.
  • Early stopping. max_hat_s stops the path after the first solution with more nonzero coefficients.
  • The results. λpath, βpath (sparse vectors) and β0path, with the passes and the convergence of coordinate descent at each level. coef(path) gives all coefficients as a sparse matrix, coef(path, λ) the coefficients at any $\lambda$ in the range of the path, interpolated linearly in $\lambda$ between levels as glmnet does, and predict(path, X) the fitted values along the path. fit(LassoPath, X, y) is the same as LassoPath(X, y).
  • Refitting. refit_lasso_path refits least squares on each distinct support of the path.

Choosing λ

CVLassoPath(X, y) chooses the level by $K$-fold cross-validation (nfolds = 10), with the options and keywords of LassoPath. The path on all the data gives the levels, the path of each fold is computed on the other folds at those levels, and the mean squared error on the held-out fold estimates the prediction error at each level; standardization and the intercept are computed on each training fold. cvm averages the fold errors weighted by the fold sizes and cvse is its standard error, as in glmnet; λmin minimizes cvm, and λ1se is the largest level whose cvm is within cvse of the minimum, the one-standard-error rule. coef(cv) and predict(cv, X) use the solution at λ1se, or at λmin with λ = :λmin. The folds are a balanced random assignment drawn from rng, or the fold labels folds, for example blocked folds for dependent data. They run as tasks on the available threads, and the result does not depend on the number of threads.

The information criteria aic, bic and ebic of a path, one value per level, choose the level without refitting:

\[\mathrm{AIC} = n\log(\mathrm{RSS}/n) + 2\,\mathrm{df}, \qquad \mathrm{BIC} = n\log(\mathrm{RSS}/n) + \log(n)\,\mathrm{df}, \qquad \mathrm{EBIC} = \mathrm{BIC} + 2\gamma\log\binom{p}{s},\]

where $\mathrm{df}$ is the number of nonzero coefficients, plus one for an intercept, and the extended BIC of Chen and Chen (2008), for $s$ nonzero coefficients among $p$, accounts for the size of the model space: path.λpath[argmin(bic(path, X, y))] is the level that BIC chooses.

Solver options

Every solver takes the options of coordinate descent as a CDOptions argument, and its fields as keywords, which replace those of the options: lasso(X, y, λ; max_iter = 10_000, opt_tol = 1e-8). The scaled and the feasible lasso take IterLassoOptions, whose field options_cd holds the options of their lasso fits.

  • The stopping rule. Coordinate descent stops when no coordinate changes by more than opt_tol (by default $10^{-7}$) in a pass, the change of coefficient $j$ measured in units of $\|y\| / \|X_j\|$, so that the result does not depend on the units of the data (relative_tol = false measures it in the units of the coefficients). It also stops after max_iter passes (by default 100,000), and then warns that the solution may be inaccurate.
  • The order. Each pass visits the coordinates in a new random order (order = :random) drawn from the keyword rng, so a seeded generator, such as rng = Xoshiro(1), reproduces the passes. order = :cyclic visits them in order.
  • Momentum. With a random order, each pass over the active set starts from a point extrapolated from the last two (Nesterov momentum). It is on by default with a random order, a SparseIterate and the built-in losses. On the designs of order_anderson_bench.jl in the benchmark snapshot it computes 50-level paths 2.7 to 6.5 times faster than the plain random order, including designs whose columns share a common factor; short solves take about as long either way. momentum = false turns it off.
  • Anderson acceleration. anderson = 5, with order = :cyclic, extrapolates from the last passes. It is faster than momentum when few passes are needed and when $n \gg p$, and much slower when the columns share a common factor.
  • Warm starts. coordinate_descent! starts from the iterate it is given. With warm_start = false it starts from zero and, for NormL1 and GroupNormL2, solves along a path of num_steps levels from $\lambda_{\max}$ down to the penalty.

coordinate_descent! and solve! run coordinate descent on a loss and a penalty directly; solve! reports the passes and whether the stopping rule was met.

The Gram form

With gram = true, lasso, sqrt_lasso and LassoPath run coordinate descent on the Gram matrix of the centered columns, $A = X^\top X / n$, instead of the residuals. A coordinate step then costs $O(p)$ instead of $O(n)$, after $O(np^2)$ operations and $p^2$ numbers of memory to form $A$. The loss, the stopping rule and so the solution are the same, to the tolerance.

Forming $A$ pays off over the many passes of a path when $n \ge p$, and almost never at one $\lambda$.
