Low-rank multivariate regression

A coefficient matrix of low rank shares a few linear combinations of the predictors across responses. For an n × p design X and n × q response matrix Y, these estimators return a p × q coefficient matrix B. The intercepts are fitted by default, without penalty, and columns keep their original scales. Inputs may be integer, Float32 or Float64; sparse inputs are converted to dense matrices. The single-penalty and rank-constrained methods return LowRankSolutions with coef, predict, residuals, nobs, deviance, and dof.

using HighDimensionalModels
s = reduced_rank(X, Y, 3)
r = select_rank(X, Y)          # estimates the noise variance by least squares
predict(r.solution, Xnew)

reduced_rank minimizes the residual sum of squares subject to a rank bound, using the truncated SVD of the least-squares fitted values. The compact SVD of the centered design handles wide and rank-deficient designs and chooses the minimum-norm coefficients. Rank zero gives the intercept-only model. select_rank minimizes RSS + μ * rank, the rank selection criterion of Bunea, She and Wegkamp (2011). Its default is μ = 2σ²(√rank(Xc) + √q)². A supplied sigma2 avoids estimating the noise variance; a supplied μ sets the threshold directly. A saturated design requires one of them, since it has no residual degrees of freedom.

Nuclear-norm regression

\[\min_{B,\beta_0}\ \frac{\|Y-1\beta_0^T-XB\|_F^2}{2n}+\lambda\|B\|_*.\]

nuclear_regression solves this convex problem by accelerated proximal gradient. ProximalBase's NuclearNorm soft-thresholds the coefficient singular values. The stopping rule checks the proximal gradient mapping at the returned coefficients. This is a penalty on B; thresholding the singular values of XB solves a different problem except for an orthonormal design.

s = nuclear_regression(X, Y, 0.1)
using ProximalAlgorithms       # optional dependency; activates the PANOC backend
s = nuclear_regression(X, Y, 0.1; solver = :panoc)
p = LowRankPath(X, Y; method = :nuclear, nλ = 30, λminratio = 0.01)
cv = CVLowRankPath(X, Y; method = :nuclear, solver = :panoc, nλ = 30, nfolds = 5)
coef(cv)                     # the minimum CV error solution
predict(cv, Xnew)

The path reuses its gradient setup and warm starts each penalty from the previous fit. For a wide design it computes gradients from X rather than forming a p × p Gram matrix. λmax = ‖Xc'Yc/n‖₂ is the zero-solution threshold. The default automatic grid starts just above it. solver = :apg remains the default. With ProximalAlgorithms installed and loaded, solver = :panoc uses its limited-memory quasi-Newton acceleration and line search. Both backends use ProximalBase's nuclear proximal operator and certify the same normalized gradient mapping at the returned coefficients.

Float64 PANOC fits reuse the loss/gradient products and final residual/singular values. Tall designs use a quadratic value with a direct residual fallback near cancellation; wide designs share one residual between value and gradient. Float32 keeps its original loss/gradient arithmetic. The internal step remains .95/L and the certificate is checked every ten iterations, plus termination. The replicated study (archive pr114-nuclear-panoc-20261026, report results/cluster-20261026/RESULTS.md) compares complete fits and paths with independent accuracy checks. PANOC helps the tested middle-penalty fits and paths, but APG is faster for the weak many-response cold fit. APG remains the default; no automatic solver-selection rule or general memory improvement is implied by those measurements.

Simultaneously sparse and low rank

sparse_nuclear_regression applies both penalties to the same coefficient matrix:

\[\min_{B,\beta_0}\ \frac{\|Y-1\beta_0^T-XB\|_F^2}{2n} +\lambda_{\mathrm{rank}}\|B\|_*+\lambda_{\mathrm{sparse}}P(B).\]

With sparsity = :entry, the default, P(B) = ∑ⱼ wⱼ ∑ₜ |B[j,t]|, the sum of absolute entries with row weights. With sparsity = :row, P(B) = ∑ⱼ wⱼ ‖B[j,:]‖₂, selecting predictors across responses. weights must contain one positive finite value per predictor and defaults to ones. Both levels may be zero. The intercepts remain unpenalized and columns keep their original scales.

The solver is ProximalSolvers' Condat–Vũ splitting, with smooth least squares, the weighted sparsity proximal operator, and a dual nuclear-norm step. Its vector iterates are reshaped for ProximalBase's NuclearNorm. This splitting solves the sum of the penalties; applying their two proximal operators in succession would generally solve a different problem.

s = sparse_nuclear_regression(X, Y, 0.1, 0.05; sparsity = :row)
s.gap, s.rank, s.rank_tol, s.activerows, s.nnz
predict(s, Xnew)

The returned SparseNuclearSolution records the coefficients, intercepts, residuals, objective, exact entry/row support, and convergence diagnostics. All describe the returned B. Its numerical rank counts singular values above rank_tol = max(√eps(T) σ₁(B), ‖B - B_h‖₂), where B_h is the last nuclear proximal point. The solver uses residual balancing without averaging to keep that point paired with the returned iterate. Balancing is disabled when the nuclear level is zero. rank_tol exposes the scale of the diagnostic; small positive singular values need not represent recovered components. coef, predict, residuals, nobs and deviance apply. No dof, aic, bic or lowrank_ic is supplied for this estimator.

Certificate and zero region

For centered data, put R = Yc - Xc*B and Θ = R/n. A dual feasible point satisfies Xc'Θ = U + V, with ‖U‖₂ ≤ λ_rank and either |V[j,t]| ≤ λ_sparse*w[j] or ‖V[j,:]‖₂ ≤ λ_sparse*w[j]. The solver's nuclear dual supplies U. Set V = Xc'R/n - U and choose the largest t ∈ [0,1] that puts tV in its sparsity ball. The nuclear dual is also checked for roundoff at its boundary. The dual objective is

\[D(t\Theta)=\frac{t\langle R,Y_c\rangle}{n} -\frac{t^2\|R\|_F^2}{2n}.\]

gap = (objective - D)/max(objective, floatmin(T)) bounds relative objective error up to floating point accuracy. Nonfinite certificates or material violations of weak duality give Inf; negative roundoff is clipped to zero. Condat–Vũ stops on this gap, and converged requires gap ≤ tol. r_norm, s_norm, eps_pri and eps_dual also record the solver's KKT residuals and tolerances. A gap tolerance does not bound coefficient error on an ill-conditioned or unidentified design.

When λ_sparse = 0, the certificate scales U = Xc'R/n into the spectral ball. When λ_rank = 0, it uses U = 0. With both levels zero, the solver uses its own KKT stopping rule and reports gap = NaN. A design that is zero after centering returns the intercept-only fit directly.

Writing C = Xc'Yc/n, zero coefficients are optimal exactly when C = U + V for elements of the two dual balls. The marginal thresholds are ‖C‖₂ and the weighted maximum absolute entry or row norm of C. Either marginal threshold alone suffices. The zero region also includes pairs with both levels below those thresholds.

Tuning two penalties

grid = PenaltyGrid(sparse_nuclear_regression, X, Y;
  nλ = (5, 5), λminratio = (0.01, 0.01), sparsity = :row)
grid.names                     # (:rank, :sparse)
grid.solutions[2, 3]            # λ1[2] is rank; λ2[3] is sparsity
cv = CVPenaltyGrid(sparse_nuclear_regression, X, Y;
  λ1 = grid.λ1, λ2 = grid.λ2, sparsity = :row, nfolds = 5)
cv.λmin, cv.cvm, cv.cvse
coef(cv)                       # the minimum-error full-data fit
predict(cv, Xnew)

PenaltyGrid stores a concrete matrix of solutions. Automatic axes start at each marginal threshold times 1 + √eps and decrease on a log scale. Explicit axes must be nonempty, decreasing, finite and nonnegative; zeros and repeated levels are allowed. Fits proceed down columns, starting each column from the head of the previous one. Primal/dual vectors and both step sizes are copied at each branch; solutions do not share mutable state.

CVPenaltyGrid uses the full-data numerical axes in every fold and recomputes preprocessing from training rows alone. Each fold has its own seeded generator and workspaces, including mutable SVD caches. threads controls fold parallelism. Explicit folds supply row labels, as for CVLowRankPath. cvm is mean squared error per response, weighted by fold sizes; cvse is the weighted standard error described in its docstring. A pair with a nonconverged full-data or training fit gets NaN and is excluded. An empty eligible set raises an error. Ties go to the first pair in column-major order, preferring larger levels. There is no two-dimensional one-standard-error rule. fit(PenaltyGrid, sparse_nuclear_regression, X, Y; ...) and the corresponding fit(CVPenaltyGrid, ...) are also available.

This combination is useful when a shared response subspace and sparse coefficients are plausible, with prediction used to assess the tradeoff. It has no fixed rank bound. In contrast, sparse_reduced_rank imposes a rank constraint and solves a nonconvex problem. The additive model lowrank_plus_sparse fits B = L + S with separate low-rank and sparse components. Task-specific designs are not supported by this estimator. An entry invisible to a task's loss still enters the nuclear norm, so removing zero or constant columns within tasks, as the multitask data layer does, would change the problem.

Combining convex penalties need not improve sample complexity over the better individual penalty. For noiseless matrix recovery from independent Gaussian linear measurements of the vectorized matrix, Oymak, Jalali, Fazel, Eldar and Hassibi (2015) give lower bounds for combinations of structure-inducing norms, including sparse and low-rank models. Those measurement assumptions differ from the shared-design regression operator here. The result cautions against a universal recovery claim; this implementation makes none. See examples/14_sparse_nuclear.jl for a small prediction comparison with nuclear regression and row lasso.

Low-rank-plus-sparse regression

lowrank_plus_sparse fits a shared low-rank component and sparse departures in the coefficient matrix:

\[\min_{L,S,\beta_0}\ \frac{\|Y-1\beta_0^T-X(L+S)\|_F^2}{2n} +\lambda_{\mathrm{rank}}\|L\|_*+\lambda_{\mathrm{sparse}}P(S).\]

The nuclear penalty acts on L; the sparsity penalty acts on S. With sparsity = :entry, P(S) = ∑ⱼ wⱼ ∑ₜ |S[j,t]|. With :row, P(S) = ∑ⱼ wⱼ ‖S[j,:]‖₂. weights contains one positive finite predictor weight per row of S, defaulting to ones. Observation, task and domain weights are not supported. The loss sums over responses and divides by n, without dividing by the number of responses q.

Centering removes the unpenalized intercepts. Columns retain their original scales, and intercept = ȳ - (L + S)'x̄. Sparse inputs are converted to dense matrices. A design that is zero after centering gives the intercept-only fit.

s = lowrank_plus_sparse(X, Y, 0.1, 0.05; sparsity = :entry)
s.L, s.S, s.B                  # B = L + S
s.gap, s.lowrank_residual, s.sparse_residual
predict(s, Xnew)               # uses the total B

LowRankPlusSparseSolution stores independent component and total matrices, intercepts, residuals, the objective and convergence diagnostics. rank counts singular values of B above the reported rank_tol = √eps(T) σ₁(B). lowrank_rank records the rank retained by the last nuclear proximal step. sparse_nnz and sparse_rows describe the exact nonzero entries and rows of S. coef, predict, residuals, nobs and deviance use the total fit. No dof, aic, bic or lowrank_ic is supplied.

This objective differs from sparse_nuclear_regression, where both penalties act on one matrix. It also differs from sparse_reduced_rank, which imposes a rank bound and solves a nonconvex problem. Here neither component has a fixed rank or support constraint, and the total B can be dense and full rank.

Solver and certificate

Restarted accelerated proximal gradient operates on the product (L, S). Its step is 1/(2‖Xc‖²_op/n): both components see the same loss gradient. ProximalBase's NuclearNorm updates L; NormL1 or GroupNormL2, with explicit predictor weights, updates S. Momentum resets at each new pair of penalty levels.

For R = Yc - Xc*(L+S) and C_R = Xc'R/n, the dual feasible set is the intersection ‖C_R‖_op ≤ λ_rank and d_w(C_R) ≤ λ_sparse, where d_w(C) = maxⱼₜ |C[j,t]|/wⱼ for entry sparsity and d_w(C) = maxⱼ ‖C[j,:]‖₂/wⱼ for row sparsity. Set

\[t=\min\left(1,\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{rank}}}{\|C_R\|_{\mathrm{op}}}, \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sparse}}}{d_w(C_R)}\right),\qquad D=\frac{t\langle R,Y_c\rangle}{n}-\frac{t^2\|R\|_F^2}{2n},\]

omitting a ratio with a zero denominator. Then tR/n is dual feasible. The reported gap = (objective-D)/max(objective, floatmin(T)) bounds relative objective error up to floating-point accuracy. Nonfinite values or material violations of weak duality give Inf; negative roundoff is clipped to zero. The solver checks this gap every few iterations and at return. For positive levels, converged requires gap ≤ tol. The objective and gap are recomputed at the returned components. lowrank_residual and sparse_residual are the Frobenius norms of their proximal-gradient mappings at that same point, using the product-space step above. A small gap does not bound coefficient or component error.

On a zero-penalty axis, the unpenalized component absorbs the minimum-norm least-squares coefficient, computed by the design SVD. If λ_rank = 0, L receives that coefficient and S = 0, including when both levels are zero. If only λ_sparse = 0, L = 0 and S receives it. These fits report gap = NaN and check the least-squares normal equations instead. lowrank_rank uses a numerical singular-value cutoff on these axes.

Zero pair, reductions and tuning

Let C₀ = Xc'Yc/n. The zero pair is optimal exactly when both λ_rank ≥ ‖C₀‖_op and λ_sparse ≥ d_w(C₀). Exceeding just one reference does not suffice. Automatic axes start just above these two references; only their upper-right corner is guaranteed to be zero. Neither upper edge is a pure-component path.

For a nuclear-only optimum N, a strict inequality λ_sparse > d_w(Xc'*(Yc-Xc*N)/n) forces S = 0 in an additive optimum. For a sparse-only optimum A, λ_rank > ‖Xc'*(Yc-Xc*A)/n‖_op forces L = 0. Conservative sufficient scales for a pure-component optimum are λ_sparse ≥ λ_rank/minimum(w) and λ_rank ≥ λ_sparse*sqrt(q)*norm(w) (entry) or λ_rank ≥ λ_sparse*norm(w) (row), respectively. Use strict margins to force the absent component; equality can permit multiple splits.

grid = PenaltyGrid(lowrank_plus_sparse, X, Y;
  sparsity = :entry, nλ = (5, 5), λminratio = (0.03, 0.03))
cv = CVPenaltyGrid(lowrank_plus_sparse, X, Y;
  λ1 = grid.λ1, λ2 = grid.λ2, sparsity = :entry, nfolds = 5)
s = cv.grid.solutions[cv.index]
cv.λmin, s.lowrank_rank, s.sparse_nnz
predict(cv, Xnew)

λ1 is the rank penalty and λ2 the sparsity penalty. Both component warm starts are copied at each grid branch, and returned fits do not share their arrays. Each CV training fold recomputes centering from its own rows and has separate proximal workspaces. The numerical axes, including automatic axes, come from the full data. cvm is prediction MSE per response, weighted by held-out row counts, with the cvse convention of CVPenaltyGrid. A pair is eligible only if its full-data and every training-fold fit converge. Nonconverged pairs have NaN scores; an empty eligible set raises an error.

Identifiability

Training fitted values are unique. The total B is unique when the centered design has full column rank. The components require a separate identification argument: even for a scalar coefficient with equal penalty levels, every split L = αB, S = (1-α)B, 0 ≤ α ≤ 1, of an optimal total is optimal. Agreement between solver starts does not prove component uniqueness. Prediction accuracy does not imply recovery of the two components.

The estimator imposes no incoherence, orthogonality, support or size constraints. Chandrasekaran, Sanghavi, Parrilo and Willsky (2011) and Hsu, Kakade and Zhang (2011) give conditions for identification and recovery in the related problem of decomposing an observed matrix into low-rank and sparse parts. Those matrix-decomposition guarantees do not establish recovery for this regression operator or its row penalty.

Task-specific designs are not supported. Coefficients invisible to one task's loss still enter the coupled nuclear penalty. Dropping each task's zero or constant predictors would change the objective. See examples/16_lowrank_plus_sparse.jl for a small prediction comparison on one low-rank-plus-sparse draw.

Row-sparse reduced-rank regression

\[\min_{\operatorname{rank}(B)\leq r,\beta_0}\ \frac{\|Y-1\beta_0^T-XB\|_F^2}{2n} +\lambda\sum_{j=1}^p w_j\|B_{j,:}\|_2.\]

sparse_reduced_rank uses the factorization B = AV', V'V = I, alternating exact row coordinate descent for the group-lasso block and an orthogonal Procrustes update for the response subspace, as in Chen and Huang (2012). The initialization is reduced-rank least squares. With adaptive = true, w is the inverse row norm of that fit to power gamma (default 2), with a sqrt(eps(T)) floor. Explicit positive weights override this choice. The problem is nonconvex: converged means the blocks have stabilized, not that a global optimum was found. block_solver = :fista uses restarted block FISTA for the weighted group-lasso block, retaining the same response update and KKT stopping conditions. block_solver = :cd remains the default. Either choice applies to paths and cross-validation. After 100 unsuccessful first-order KKT checks (100 CD sweeps or 1000 FISTA iterations), each block solve finishes with guarded Newton steps on the nonzero rows, including later blocks in that fit; inner_max_iter bounds first-order iterations only. The defaults for tol and inner_tol are 1e-7 and 1e-9 in Float64, each with a 100eps(T) floor; explicit tolerances are unchanged. sparse_reduced_rank warns when it does not converge and reports when rounding of an ill-conditioned Gram matrix can limit accuracy; paths retain their convergence flags without warnings.

s = sparse_reduced_rank(X, Y, 3, 0.1; adaptive = true)
p = LowRankPath(X, Y; method = :sparse, rank = 3, block_solver = :fista, nλ = 30)
best = p.solutions[argmin(lowrank_ic(p; criterion = :bic))]
cv = CVLowRankPath(X, Y; method = :sparse, rank = 3, adaptive = true)

A zero fit is restarted from the RRR initialization at the next level, since zero factors otherwise form an absorbing point of alternating updates. The rank bound is fixed along a sparse penalty path; compare candidate bounds using the same explicit folds in separate CVLowRankPath calls.

Sparse singular vectors

sparse_svd_regression(X, Y, r) also allows sparse response loadings. It uses one round of exclusive extraction from the SVD of the RRR coefficients, with a rank-one weighted lasso in each exclusive response layer. The selected components need not remain orthogonal. Each layer chooses its penalty by BIC, BICP or AIC and may select a zero component. This is the RSSVD structure of Chen, Chan and Stenseth (2012), implemented without Kronecker design matrices. The return value contains the solution, individual components, component penalty grids λ, and scores.

ss = sparse_svd_regression(X, Y, 3; nλ = 30, criterion = :bic)
predict(ss.solution, Xnew)
cv = CVLowRankPath(X, Y; method = :sparse_svd, ranks = 0:3, nλ = 20)

For this CV path, each training fold selects component penalties by BIC, then held-out prediction error selects the rank bound. Every fold recomputes its centering, initial fit, and adaptive weights. Convergence is local.

Tuning and information criteria

LowRankPath(...; method = :rank, ranks = 0:5) shares the design and fitted SVDs across rank constraints. Fixed-rank results reuse those decompositions and use a small QR/SVD core for coefficient rank; residuals are computed once. Selected-design rank, numerical coefficient rank, and exact degrees of freedom retain their definitions, including custom fitting cutoffs and zero fits. lowrank_ic returns scores on the normalized scale log(RSS) + penalty * df/(n*q). AIC uses 2 and BIC uses log(n*q); BICP uses 2log(n*q) for row-sparse regression and 2log(p*q) for RSSVD. These are the scales in rrpack. Additive constants do not affect selection. The StatsAPI methods aic(solution) and bic(solution) use n*q * log(RSS/(n*q)) + penalty * df, matching the lasso path convention.

By default df counts the parameters of the selected rank/support model: r * (rank(X_selected) + q - r), plus q intercepts. This is an approximation for penalized estimators. For rank-constrained fits, df = :exact uses the Stein formula of Mukherjee et al. (2015); a cutoff through tied fitted singular values receives an infinite score. RSSVD component scores count nnz(u) + nnz(v) - 1.

CVLowRankPath uses a common rank or penalty grid across folds and computes mean squared error per response, weighted by fold sizes. Supplied folds allow grouped or blocked validation. A nonconverged fold fit raises an error. For a rank path, request ranks that are estimable in every training fold.

rrpack 0.1-14 interchanges its BIC/BICP selection labels inside rssvd.eea. The R oracle, test/data/lowrank/oracle.R, aligns the actual BIC column explicitly; the methods here use the criterion names according to the formulas above.

The low-rank study (snapshot) records replicated timing and profiles; the lowrank group of the benchmark harness measures the current fits. The test suite checks the fits against rrpack fits saved by the R oracle. rrpack uses unscaled squared losses: its penalty is n * λ for the objectives here. Its adaptive nuclear-norm estimator shrinks the fitted-value singular values, so it is not an oracle for the coefficient nuclear penalty; the latter has an exact orthonormal-design test and a proximal-gradient optimality check.

The sparse-fit setup optimization reuses X'X/n and X'Y/n within each fit and across penalty levels in a path. The FISTA step scale comes from the existing design SVD. Coefficient rank, degrees of freedom and residuals are computed from thin factors with conservative dense fallbacks. The objective, weights, support/DF semantics and stopping rules in that study were unchanged. CD results were unchanged and FISTA results agreed within the stopping tolerance. CD remains the default block solver. The replicated optimization study (archive pr112-sparse-setup-20261026, report results/cluster-20261026/FINAL.md) used 20 shared independent inputs per design on an earlier dependency stack and was not rerun at this revision. At the middle penalty, paired public-fit candidate/baseline ratios were 0.854 (CD) and 0.860 (FISTA) for many responses, and 0.854 for wide FISTA. Wide CD was inconclusive at 0.962, with an interval reaching 1.003; small CD regressed at 1.016. Complete paths improved in every retained design/solver. FISTA was useful for ill-conditioned and rank-deficient inputs, while CD was faster on strong/middle wide inputs. The study establishes no universal solver or default.
